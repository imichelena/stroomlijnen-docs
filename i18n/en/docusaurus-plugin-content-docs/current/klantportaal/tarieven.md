# Tariff structure

A per-kWh tariff at a charging point is not an arbitrary number. It is the sum of a few components, and it helps to know them — especially if you compare tariffs from different providers.

## The components

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Energy procurement** | What the electricity itself costs at the moment of charging |
| **Energy tax** | Levy per kWh, depending on your connection and consumption |
| **Grid tariffs** | Costs of transport, included in the tariff |
| **Operation** | Maintenance, management, fault response, platform, depreciation of the hardware |
| **Margin** | What remains for the party operating the infrastructure |

With a fixed tariff, the first three factors are combined into one price. With a dynamic tariff, the first component moves with the market.

## Why it differs per site

Two sites can have a different tariff, even with the same provider, because:

- the connection is different (small consumption versus large consumption, different energy tax)
- the consumption profile is different (continuous versus peak load)
- there may or may not be own generation or a battery
- the site has different costs (rent for the space, service)

A tariff is therefore not a property of the provider but of the **combination** of site and contract.

## The cultural tariff

For the cultural sector, Stroomlijnen works with a cultural tariff. It is not a discount for the sake of a discount: it is tied to the agreement that the **ERE revenue** from the charging sessions benefits the sector (via Het Cultuurfonds). See [Revenue model](/en/docs/pandeneigenaren/verdienmodel).

:::note No amounts in this knowledge base
Tariffs change annually and differ per site and contract. That is why no kWh prices are listed here. You will find your tariff in the Customer Portal under **Tariffs** and in your agreement.
:::

## Comparing with other providers

When comparing, watch these four things — they usually explain the difference:

1. **Is the tariff including or excluding VAT?** A difference of 21% is often presented as "cheaper".
2. **Are there start-up costs per session?** At ten sessions a month, that adds up.
3. **Are there time or parking costs?** Especially relevant if vehicles stand idle for long periods.
4. **Does the tariff also apply when it is busy?** A tariff that only applies when nobody else is charging is not a tariff.

## What a tariff does *not* cover

- **Grid reinforcement** — if your connection is too small, that is a separate investment
- **Installation of the connection** — cabling and circuits in the building
- **Subsidies** — those reduce the investment, not the kWh tariff
