# Revenue model

A location can earn from charging. How much, and in what way, depends on the contract form. There are three common models — and they differ not only in revenue but also in **who carries the risks**.

## The three models

### 1. Operation by the CPO

The CPO installs, manages and invoices; you receive a fee per kWh delivered.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** | An agreed share per kWh, or a fixed amount per period |
| **Work for you** | Virtually none |
| **Investment** | None, or partly via subsidy |
| **Risk** | With the CPO: occupancy, faults, tariffs |

This is the model most locations choose, simply because it demands little attention.

### 2. Own operation

You set the tariff, you bear the costs, you keep the margin.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** | The difference between your tariff and your costs |
| **Work for you** | Tariff management, customer questions, contracts with users |
| **Investment** | Partly or entirely yourself |
| **Risk** | With you: if nobody charges, the costs are still there |

This pays off at locations with heavy and predictable use, or where you want to use the tariff as an instrument (for example free charging for guests).

### 3. Capacity rental

You rent out the space and the capacity to an operator for a fixed amount, regardless of use.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Revenue** | Fixed amount per period |
| **Work for you** | Virtually none |
| **Risk** | With the tenant |

Attractive if you want certainty and do not want to share in growth.

:::note Why there are no amounts here
Revenue per kWh and per month differs by location, contract duration and year. Figures in a knowledge base age and are then used in negotiations where they no longer apply. Your concrete proposal comes from a calculation on your own data; it comes with a substantiation, not a little table.
:::

## The ERE revenue

A special arrangement applies to the cultural sector. Charging sessions yield **EREs** (renewable energy units). At Stroomlijnen, the revenue from these goes **to the sector**, via Het Cultuurfonds — not to the location and not to us as profit.

That is the core of the culture tariff: it is not "cheaper because we are nice", but a model in which the revenue from the energy flows back to the sector that has to make the transition.

:::note What you need to provide
To be able to register EREs, the generation and the consumption must be demonstrable. With an existing solar panel installation, an extra meter may be needed for this. Discuss this during the intake; afterwards it is more difficult.
:::

## What you pay yourself

Even in the most favourable model there are items for you:

| Item | Who pays |
|---|---|
| Standing charge of your connection | **You** — you already pay this now |
| Electrical modification in the building | **You** |
| Grid upgrade (if needed) | **You** |
| Access, site, security | **You** |
| Energy for the sessions | Passed on to the user |
| Maintenance, management, faults, platform | The operator |

Be suspicious of a provider who says you pay "nothing": usually those costs are worked into the kWh tariff, and then the driver pays them.

## Where the money really comes from

A charging point earns from **volume**, not from a high margin per kWh. That has a few consequences:

- A location where one truck charges per month will never earn back the hardware.
- A location where a company stands every week can do very well.
- That is why *visibility* matters: if production managers do not know that you can charge, nobody will come.

The latter is also the reason we actively recommend locations to productions and carriers, instead of only putting them on a map.
