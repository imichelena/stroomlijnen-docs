# Why Theaters Are the Hidden Gem of EV Charging Infrastructure

Everyone's looking at highway rest stops, shopping centers, and dedicated charging hubs for EV infrastructure. But the best charging locations might be the ones nobody's thinking about: **theaters and event venues.**

<!-- truncate -->

## The Grid Connection Problem

The biggest bottleneck for EV charging infrastructure isn't the chargers themselves — it's the **grid connection.** A 350 kW DC fast charger needs a serious electrical connection. For a site with multiple chargers serving electric trucks, you're looking at 1,000–2,000 kVA of available grid capacity.

Getting a new heavy grid connection in the Netherlands can take **2–5 years** and cost **€200,000–500,000+**, depending on location and capacity. Grid operators (Liander, Enexis, Stedin) have long waiting lists, especially in congested areas.

This is the reason many planned charging hubs are stuck in development limbo. The chargers are ready, the software is ready, the demand is there — but the grid connection isn't.

## Theaters Already Have What You Need

Here's what makes theaters special: they already have **massive grid connections.**

A mid-size theater typically has 400–800 kVA of grid capacity. A large venue or concert hall? 1,000–2,000+ kVA. This powers the lighting rigs, sound systems, HVAC, and stage machinery that a production requires.

But here's the key insight: **that power is used primarily during performances — evenings and weekends.**

During the day — exactly when electric trucks need to charge — those grid connections sit largely idle. The theater might draw 50–100 kVA for HVAC and office operations. The remaining capacity? Unused.

### The Numbers

| Time | Theater Grid Use | Available for Charging |
|------|-----------------|----------------------|
| 08:00–17:00 (weekday) | ~50–100 kVA | **700–1,400 kVA** |
| 17:00–23:00 (show time) | 400–800 kVA | 200–400 kVA |
| 23:00–08:00 (night) | ~20 kVA | **780–1,480 kVA** |

A single theater can support **3–8 simultaneous DC fast chargers** during daytime hours without any grid expansion.

## No Waiting, No Digging

By partnering with theaters, we bypass the grid connection queue entirely:

- **No new grid connection application** — the capacity exists
- **No excavation work** — the electrical infrastructure runs to the building
- **No 3-year wait** — we install chargers in weeks, not years
- **Lower capital cost** — we pay the theater for grid access rather than building our own

This is not theoretical. It's how Stroomlijnen operates. Our chargers are live at theater locations across the Netherlands, running on existing grid connections.

## Why Theaters Win Too

This isn't charity — it's a genuine revenue opportunity for venues:

### New Revenue Stream
Theaters earn a grid hosting fee for providing access to their electrical capacity during off-peak hours. This is passive income from infrastructure they've already paid for.

### Sustainability Credentials
Cultural venues are increasingly expected to demonstrate environmental commitment. Hosting EV charging — especially for electric trucks — is tangible, visible sustainability action.

### Parking Utilization
Theater parking lots sit empty during the day. Charging trucks don't interfere with evening performances — they're gone by the time the audience arrives.

### Community Integration
A theater with EV chargers becomes a community hub — not just a venue but a practical piece of infrastructure that serves the local economy.

## The Fleet Operator Perspective

For fleet managers, theater charging locations offer:

- **Urban/suburban locations** — theaters are typically in or near city centers, where trucks make deliveries
- **Predictable availability** — daytime hours are reliably available
- **High power** — the grid connections support fast charging
- **Safe, well-lit locations** — theaters invest in their surroundings
- **No competition with passenger cars** — these aren't shopping center chargers with 15 cars queuing for 2 plugs

## Scaling the Model

The Netherlands has approximately 600 professional theaters and concert venues, plus hundreds of event spaces, conference centers, and cultural venues. If even 30% adopted EV charging:

- **~180 new charging locations** without any grid expansion
- **~700–1,400 new charging points** for electric trucks
- Estimated **500–1,000 GWh per year** of additional charging capacity

This won't solve the entire Dutch truck charging challenge — but it's a significant, fast, and cost-effective contribution. And unlike purpose-built charging hubs, these locations can be operational in months, not years.

## The Bigger Picture

The energy transition isn't just about building new infrastructure. It's about **using existing infrastructure smarter.** Theaters are one example, but the principle applies broadly:

- Stadiums and arenas (massive grid, used intermittently)
- Schools and universities (high grid capacity, used business hours only)
- Industrial sites with off-shift capacity
- Any building with an oversized grid connection relative to its average demand

The grid connection is the bottleneck. Finding buildings that already have the connection — and need it only part of the time — is the shortcut.
