Managing Grid Congestion on Dutch Distribution Networks

A guide to telemetry integration, capacity limitation orders, and real-time curtailment with regional Netbeheerders (Liander, Enexis, Stedin).

calendar_today Updated: 14 Oct 2024 • groups Stroomlijnen Grid Integration Team • Audience: CPO Engineers & Energy Planners

In the Netherlands, physical transformer saturation and distribution grid bottlenecks managed by regional distribution system operators (DSOs—specifically Liander, Enexis, and Stedin) have made unconditional grid connections rare for heavy fast-charging depots and fleet hubs. Transmission transmission system operator TenneT enforces localized strict feed-in and take-off margins across high-voltage feed points.

To accommodate high-power commercial electric transport charging without triggering local feeder tripping, Charge Point Operators (CPOs) must implement deterministic telemetry links capable of reacting instantaneously to dynamic network curtailment orders.

info
Statutory Netcode Compliance

Under the updated Netcode Elektriciteit (ACM Besluit 2023/184), all non-firm charging sites over 100 kW must respond to Capacity Limitation Orders (CapLO) within 15.0 seconds of receiving telecontrol dispatch payloads. Failure to shed downstream load results in automated substation circuit opening by netbeheerder switchgear.

Regional DSO Dispatch Protocols

Grid congestion management operates on a dual-plane telemetry architecture. Day-ahead and intraday flexibility scheduling is brokered through the pan-operator GOPACS clearing platform, while emergency physical network security orders rely on direct telecontrol telemetry lines directly routed into on-premise depot edge gateways.

When a netbeheerder flags an active bottleneck at a medium-voltage substation feeder, dispatch signals propagate according to the matrix detailed below:

Signal Type Protocol / Format Latency Req. Fallback Default
Emergency Curtailment (CapLO) IEC 60870-5-104 < 5.0 seconds Throttle to 6A safe minimum
Day-Ahead Congestion Bid GOPACS XML / REST v2 15-minute intervals Scheduled profile retention
Dynamic Capacity Window OpenADR 2.0b EiEvent < 15.0 seconds Cluster ceiling enforcement
Phase Imbalance Alert Modbus TCP / SunSpec < 1.0 second Automated phase-rotation cut

Firmware-Level Curtailment Hierarchy

When edge nodes receive multi-source limits simultaneously, the internal dispatch engine must evaluate charging quotas through a three-tier hardline priority pipeline:

  • 1
    Tier 1: Emergency Physical Cutoff (DSO CapLO)

    Overrides all active commercial sessions. Executes instantaneous shedding down to guaranteed circuit ceiling or cold disconnect to prevent thermal runaway of the local transformer step-down.

  • 2
    Tier 2: Contractual Firm Capacity Envelope (CBC / TDBC)

    Governed by the dynamic Capacity-Based Contract agreed with Stedin or Liander. Dynamic shedding allocates available amperes proportionally across connectors via OCPP 2.0.1 SetChargingProfile.

  • 3
    Tier 3: User Scheduled Charging & Fleet SLA Priorities

    Fleet depot schedules, VIP priority passes, and SOC-based balancing. Operates entirely strictly within the residual ceiling remaining after Tiers 1 and 2 are satisfied.

Fallback & Failsafe Behaviors

In cases where continuous heartbeat telemetry with the regional DSO endpoint drops for longer than 45 consecutive seconds (the Netbeheerder Watchdog Timeout), edge charging controllers must autonomously revert to the pre-contracted emergency floor without external prompting.

Standard commercial configurations throttle all active AC connectors to 6 Amperes single-phase and cap DC fast charging stations at a maximum draw of 25 kW aggregated across the main point of delivery (Overdrachtspunt).
